Multi-tenant MSSP operations

In an MSSP, you don't run one SOC — you run many at once, each a different customer with its own estate, risk profile, and SLA. I operated across multiple enterprise tenants simultaneously, onboarding new customers end to end: standing up log collection, normalizing dozens of source types per tenant, building customer-specific detections, and keeping each tenant's data and dashboards strictly isolated. I served as the L2 escalation point — the analyst incidents got routed to when L1 needed a decision — and as shift lead coordinating real-time coverage and handoffs.

Deploying & correlating the full security stack

I didn't just consume alerts — I deployed the platforms that generate them and wired them together so a signal in one tool enriched every other:

  • SIEM & XDR: deployed and tuned LogRhythm SIEM and Stellar Cyber XDR — led tool migrations, onboarded 85+ log sources, and built parsing/normalization for non-standard formats.
  • EDR: rolled out SentinelOne and Kaspersky EDR across endpoint fleets, tuned policy/exclusions, and used them for containment (host isolation, kill/quarantine) during live incidents.
  • Network — NGFW, NAC, NDR, IDS/IPS: deployed and managed FortiGate, Cisco Meraki, and Palo Alto firewall policy; ran network access control with Cisco ISE and Forescout (802.1X posture, profiling, and segmentation); and integrated Darktrace NDR and IDS/IPS (Suricata, Snort) so east-west and north-south traffic fed the same correlation layer, alongside a Radware WAF.
  • Cross-platform correlation: built the integrations that let firewall, EDR, NDR, NAC, and identity telemetry land in one timeline — so a firewall block, an EDR detonation, and an NDR beacon on the same host correlated into a single, confirmed incident instead of three orphan alerts.

Detection engineering — the scenarios

I engineered 80+ correlation rules and custom regex-based triage filters mapped to MITRE ATT&CK, cutting the false-positive rate by 60% and mean triage time from 30 minutes to under 10. The detections spanned the full kill chain:

  • Initial access: phishing with malicious attachments/links, password spray and credential stuffing, brute force against exposed RDP/VPN, and anomalous first-time geolocations.
  • Execution & persistence: malicious Office macros, suspicious PowerShell/encoded commands, scheduled-task and run-key persistence, new service creation.
  • Credential access & privilege escalation: Kerberoasting, LSASS access, suspicious privilege grants and group changes, MFA-fatigue / push-bombing patterns.
  • Lateral movement & C2: pass-the-hash and SMB/WMI/RDP pivoting, and command-and-control beaconing detected by periodicity/jitter, rare user agents, and DNS tunneling.
  • Exfiltration & impact: large or off-hours outbound transfers, data staging, and ransomware precursors — shadow-copy deletion and mass file-rename bursts — caught before encryption spread.

The triage-time win came from filter design, not headcount: regex filters that auto-suppressed known-benign noise (vulnerability-scanner traffic, health checks, service accounts on their expected paths); alert enrichment that auto-attached asset owner, user, geo, and threat-intel reputation so an analyst saw the whole story without console-hopping; and multi-signal correlation that collapsed related events into one case — a firewall deny, an EDR detection, and an NDR beacon on the same host became a single high-confidence incident instead of three separate alerts to chase.

Threat hunting & threat intelligence

Beyond alert-driven work, I ran proactive hunts — hypothesis-led sweeps for anomalous authentication, lateral movement, and dormant C2 that signature-based alerting misses. I integrated and operationalized threat-intelligence feeds (open-source and commercial reputation/IOC sources), and ran an IOC lifecycle across tenants: an indicator confirmed in one customer's environment was vetted and pushed as protection to the other seven, so one detection hardened the whole book of business. I fed 45+ unique IOCs into shared intelligence in the process.

Incident response & digital forensics

I led 15+ end-to-end investigations, including multiple ransomware engagements. That meant scoping the blast radius across the customer estate, containing affected hosts via EDR isolation and firewall/NAC quarantine, and doing the forensics: memory dumps, disk imaging, timeline reconstruction, and IOC extraction. I delivered root-cause-analysis reports that named the initial vector and the control gaps that let it through, and coordinated remediation with each customer's internal team — the bridge between "alert fired" and "won't happen again."

Leadership, playbooks & PAM

As shift lead I owned real-time SOC operations and was the L2 escalation point for the floor. I authored 25+ MITRE ATT&CK-mapped playbooks covering 12 threat tactics with automated alert-enrichment workflows, and ran knowledge transfer and mentoring for new analysts so response didn't depend on tribal knowledge. I also deployed BeyondTrust PAM internally — full session recording, just-in-time access provisioning, and credential vaulting integrated with Active Directory — eliminating standing privileged access through ephemeral, least-privilege workflows.
